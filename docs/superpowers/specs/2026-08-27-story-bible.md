# Valheim: The Saga — story bible

Written 2026-08-27 at `0.221.12-run.alpha74`, rewritten the same day to carry
the WHOLE story at the owner's request. This is the source of truth for the
saga's fiction. If a mechanic and this document disagree, one of them is a bug.

---

## The premise

**Nothing in this world can make its own light.**

Valheim is the tenth world — where Odin sends the dead, the defeated, the
put-away. Everything here is *after* its life: the greydwarves are animated
deadwood, Bonemass is corpses that stood up, the mountains froze, Yagluth's
plains are a ruin. Dead things do not generate light. They can only take it.

**The deer are the exception.** Eikthyr's herd carries original light —
sparks from before this world was a prison. The herd banks its light by day,
which is why the hunt is nocturnal: only in the dark can a light rise at all.
This is lore, not a difficulty knob, and it holds at every layer of the game.

**You arrive alive** — the first new light this world has seen in an age.
That is why the forest notices your axe, why things follow your hunt, why the
ravens have started landing near you.

**Odin did not send you to slay gods. He sent you to find out where the light
is going.** The ravens are his audit. The gods keep being in the way of the
answer — and gods only speak when defeated.

---

## The frame: you, the gods, and the ledger

**Heat** is the world noticing you. Every task done, every light taken back,
makes you burn brighter — and the tenth world rises toward the brightest
thing in it. That is why heat raises the world against you AND multiplies
your score: the score is how brightly you burned, and burning bright is
never free.

**Boons are loans.** Odin advances power against the answer you owe him;
nothing granted is yours. Death lets the world collect — a boon is taken
back — and everything is repaid when the saga ends, whatever its ending.
The lobby says the rule to your face: *power must be earned here.*

**The hearth is where you tend your own light.** The homestead track is not
a tutorial — a fire, a claimed bed, a full table, a comfortable room are how
a living spark keeps itself lit in a dead world. That is why rest makes you
stronger (Tireless is EARNED by a night in your own bed) and why the saga
will not let the homestead be optional flavour. Home is the one place the
world is not taking from you.

**Homeward** exists because a light that cannot return to its hearth gutters.
The way home is never fully closed: a boss's fall earns a charge, and the
road itself opens every ten minutes.

## Act I — The Stolen Light (the whole story)

*Epigraph: Something is taking the light from the meadows. Take it back.*

You land in the Meadows and build a life: axe, hammer, shelter, fire, a bed
you claim as yours. The homestead is the act's spine — the saga's premise in
miniature, a spark tending itself.

At night, the meadows whisper. *The dark is awake. The forest is counting.*
The act's one rule is taught before it is tested: **nothing you seek walks in
the light.**

**The pale light** opens the hunt: a single free spark drifting at the edge
of the world — the one the forest never found. It cannot be caught by day.
The strip carries only a rumour ("something pale drifts far to the
north-east" — never a number; a rumour is not a bearing), and walking INTO it
takes it. Its going-out is the act's thesis stated in full:

> *It was one of the herd's — the one the forest never found. The deer give
> up their light when they fall, and the forest is always collecting. Hunt
> after dark, and take back what you can.*

**The bargain.** Eikthyr will not answer a living stranger freely; his
trophies are the price of an audience, and trophies mean hunting his herd.
So you do what the forest does — kill deer — with one difference that is the
whole moral weight of the act: *what you do with their lights is between you
and the forest.*

**The race.** A deer killed at night gives up its light where it falls. It
burns for half a minute. The forest sends its children for it — the pack at
the carcass at once, and more walking in from the dark toward the light
itself — because they are not vicious, they are STARVING: every greydwarf is
a splinter of the Elder that goes dark unless fed. You take a light by
running into it, at a sprint if you must, and the scoreboard keeps the truth
in the open: you N — forest N. Boss drums play, because this, not the god at
the end, is the fight the act is about.

**The strays.** Rarely, at night, a light kindles that no deer died for —
one the forest has not caught YET. Strays are serene: no pack, no walkers.
Finding one counts fully, and it is the one quiet mercy the act offers a
player losing the carcass races.

**The forfeit.** Let the forest take eight and the hunt is over — the step
completes, and pays nothing. The trophies are lost and must be hunted plain.
Expensive, visible, survivable. The saga does not end runs over a race; it
just refuses to reward a lost one.

**The Herald** is the herd's guardian — the one deer that is an event, named,
starred, found by real bearings because a hunt has a quarry where a rumour
does not. While it stands the herd has a protector; its fall is the loudest
light-release the meadows have ever heard. *Its fall will be heard* is
literal.

**The Gatherer** is what heard it. The biggest, oldest splinter — fat enough
to have a name — that has followed your hunt all act, letting its kin do the
carrying. The Herald's fall puts the richest prize of the act on the ground,
and it comes to collect; it finds a rival harvester standing over it instead.
It arrives announced (*The Gatherer knows what you have taken*), at night
only, and it arrives AS WHAT THE RACE MADE IT — every light you let the
forest take is health it walks in with, and its arrival line reads the ledger
out loud, lean and furious through glutted. Kill it and everything it held
goes free around its corpse, yours to gather at leisure: freed lights belong
to nobody now, and their guttering is cleanup, not a loss.

**The altar, then the god.** The freed hunt leads to Eikthyr's stones. You
hang the trophies his own herd paid for and call him down. Whether he sees a
defiler or the only one who fought the real thieves is deliberately never
answered — the ambiguity IS the story, and the saga never rules on it.

A raven lands when the pale light goes out, and again when the act turns.
It watches. It does not help.

## Acts II–VII — each a failed answer to the shortage

| Act | Title | Their answer |
|---|---|---|
| II | Where the Light Goes | **Steal it.** The Elder — a hunger wearing a tree — farms the herd through his splinters |
| III | Nothing Stays Buried | **Never let go.** The swamp hoards its dead's light; that is why nothing stays down, and Bonemass is everything that never let go |
| IV | The White Silence | **It is already gone.** The mountains froze when theirs ran out. Moder guards eggs: light that has not woken |
| V | The Golden Ruin | **Industrialise it.** Yagluth's people harvested at scale; the ruin is what remains when the harvest runs dry |
| VI | A Light to Carry | **Carry it honestly.** The dvergr keep wisps in lanterns — borrowed and returned. The only ones who got it right |
| VII | The Last Light | **Let it burn.** Where light goes to end |

**Act II, as built so far:** it opens on the premise, said once, twelve
seconds after the card — *Nothing here makes its own light. Remember that.*
You claim Eikthyr's power (a dead god's answer, hung on your wall), take
cores from the dead in the burial chambers, kill the shamans that tend the
nests (the nests themselves cannot be counted, so the story counts the
tenders), fell the forest that answers the axe — the Elder noticing his food
being cut — and follow where the stolen light was carried: the oldest trees
feed first. You raise a raft, because the
Elder does not always wait on your shore. And at night you ROB THE COURIERS:
starred, named, visibly laden — a Courier of the Elder is a greydwarf given a
burden and a title, and the brand IS the cargo. Cut one down and a Stolen
Light rises where it falls: the same race, the pack that converges now its
kin come to carry it onward. The act introduces them once, plainly, the
moment the hunt opens: "The forest moves its harvest at night." Act I raced
the forest for the deer's light; Act II robs the supply line. Haldor trades at the
forest's edge; a trader in a starving world is its own quiet mystery,
unexplored yet.

**The arc's payoff, planned:** the wisp you pocket from every race is the
same species of thing the dvergr carry in lanterns. By Act VI the player
discovers they have been collecting honestly since Act I — the dvergr's
answer, practised before it was taught. Act VII spends it.

## Named things (glossary)

- **A light / a deer's light** — original spark, wisp-bodied, rises only in
  darkness, burns ~30s unless taken by touch.
- **The pale light** — the single free spark that opens Act I. The forest
  never found it; the strays are its kin.
- **A stray** — a light the forest has not caught yet. Serene, rare, counts.
- **The Herald** — the herd's named guardian. Two stars, real bearings.
- **The Gatherer** — the oldest splinter, named, arrives fed by your failures,
  drops its hoard on death.
- **Splinters** — greydwarves: deadwood animated by the Elder, starving
  always. They carry lights; they never destroy them.
- **The wisp (item)** — a taken light, pocketed. Act VI's payoff in waiting.
- **The ravens** — Odin's audit. They witness beats; they never help.
- **Heat** — the world noticing you burn. Score is how brightly you burned.
- **Boons** — Odin's loans against the answer you owe. Death collects one.
- **The hearth** — where a living spark tends itself. Never optional flavour.

## Rules this premise imposes on content

- Light rises only in the dark, everywhere, always — lore, not tuning.
- The forest's creatures converge on light out of hunger; they carry, never
  destroy. Nothing taking a light may read as vandalism.
- The player's own light is never mechanised — it is why things notice them,
  not a resource with a number.
- Ravens appear at moments Odin would want witnessed, and never help.
- Killing a god settles that god's hoard but never solves the shortage. The
  shortage is the saga; only Act VII answers it.
- No line the world does not back: every hint, whisper and message must
  describe something that actually happens. (Paid for repeatedly: the
  trees whisper, the drifting lights, "freed lights drift toward the altar".)
- The story is told where the player is looking — at carcasses, arrivals and
  deaths — never in a wall of text. The lobby paragraph is the only
  exposition the saga allows itself.
