# The Saga, as it plays — a walkthrough

*This is the saga told as one run would play it, start to finish. It was written on 2026-10-05
from the code and the story bible, at build `1.0.16-run.2026-10-05e`. Every quoted line is one
the game actually says. The deeds between the quotes are the questline steps, written as lived
moments rather than a checklist. Acts change, and this file will drift as they do. When it
disagrees with `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-27-story-bible.md`, the bible wins. Act III is told
as designed, not as built; Acts VI to VIII are told only as far as their stand-ins go. The run
below takes up the Hunter's way, because a story has to choose; every other way would read
differently at the graves and nowhere else.*

---

## Before the first page

Nothing in this world can make its own light.

Valheim is the tenth world, the one Odin keeps for the dead, the defeated and the put-away.
Everything in it is *after* its life. The greydwarves are animated deadwood. The swamp is corpses
that would not lie down. The mountains froze. The plains are a ruin somebody else built. Dead
things do not make light. They can only take it.

The one exception was a herd of deer. Eikthyr's animals carried original light, sparks from
before the world was a prison, and they banked it by day and could only give it up in the dark.

The saga's lobby said one thing plainly before anything else began: *"The saga is played without
cheats. Power must be earned here."* Odin would lend power against an answer still owed. Every
gift on the wheel was a loan, and death would take one back. And the world would notice anyone who
burned brightly enough. That noticing had a number, called heat, and heat multiplied the score.

## Act I — The Stolen Light

*"Something is taking the light from the meadows. Take it back."*

You came ashore alive, which nothing in this world had managed in an age, and the dark noticed you
before anything else did.

So you built. The first thing was an axe, and the world handed back a bow, arrows and wood, and the
knack of using them. The second was a hammer, and with it timber and stone for a roof. Ten wood made
a workbench. You stood close to it while you worked, and a leather tunic came off it.

The meadows were thick with greylings and boar. You fed the camp on them and kept it, and leather
leggings, a helmet and a cape came for the trouble. You walked the grass picking up whatever you had
not held yet: berries, mushrooms, flint, feathers, resin. Twelve new things, because more of the same
did not count, and the larder filled with seeds.

Walls alone are not shelter, so you raised a roof six pieces wide. Under it you set a campfire, not
under a wooden floor. A cooking station went *on* the fire, not beside it. You ate three different
things at once, cooked meat and berries and mushrooms, and for the first time you were properly fed.
You built a bed under the roof and claimed it. That made the place yours, the one place in this
world it was not taking from you.

In the morning someone was standing among stones a little way from your door. *"Someone stands among
stones a little way from your door. He was not there in the dark."*

He kept seven graves. He spoke as though he had been rehearsing it for an age:

> "I buried them. Seven of a hird that came before you, and I am what is left of the eighth — kept
> here to count the stones, since nobody came for any of us.
>
> Eydís, who hunted, and whom the beasts followed. Sigrún, who mended, and called up what the ground
> held. Ulfr, who never learned how to stop. Halvard, who stood where he was put and did not move.
> Ormr, who sang the rest of us onward. Ragna, who was never once afraid of water. Dvalinn, who built
> the hall they all died in.
>
> None of them needs the name any more. Take one up and I will teach you what they knew — a little
> at a time, as the gods fall. Or take none, and go. The stones are kept either way."

You took up Eydís.

> "Eydís, then. She walked with a wolf at heel and nothing saw her go. The wolf comes when you call
> now. The rest of her comes as the gods fall."

A wolf would come to Keypad7 now, a packbrother that followed and fought. The stalk and the bow came
with her name, and they were not a loan. Death takes boons, but it would never take a way.

You settled in: two minutes at home under the roof, in the fire's warmth, near your bench and your
bed. A shield turned up by the door, and arrows. You slept through the night in your own bed, and
woke with a hot meal, a fishing rod and the boon called **Tireless**, earned by sleeping well. You
added a chair and a table until the room was comfortable, and the rested feeling lasted longer for
each. You built a chest. Then you upgraded the bench twice, with a chopping block and a tanning
rack, both inside its circle.

At night the meadows whispered over all of it.

> "The dark is awake."
> "The forest is counting."
> "Something is owed, and it is being collected."

### The errand, the kill that gave nothing, and the vigil

A raven had been circling since you landed. *"A raven has been circling since you landed. It wants
to say something."* You stood still long enough to let it land.

> "He did not send you for the antlered one. He sent you to find where the light is going."

That was the whole saga in one sentence, and the first time anyone had said it.

*"The meadows are full of deer. Take one in the daylight and see what you get."* You did. The
carcass was warm and gave you venison, and nothing else.

> "Nothing rose. They bank it while the sun is up — there is no light in a deer at noon. Come back
> when it is dark."

So you sat in the dark. *"Nothing you want walks in the light. Sit in the dark until you believe
it."* You stayed out past sundown with nothing to kill and listened. The meadows spoke three times,
and a torch and eyes used to the dark came for it.

*"A pale light kindles ahead."* At the edge of the world, somewhere off to the north-east, something
drifted. The strip never gave a number for it, only a rumour. You walked into it.

> "The pale light goes out."
>
> "So you found one still burning. There were more, once. Watch what the forest does with the
> rest." — Hugin
>
> "It was one of the herd's — the one the forest never found."
>
> "The deer give up their light when they fall, and the forest is always collecting. Hunt after
> dark, and take back what you can."

### The hunter's shade

*"Something waits near your bed at night. It was a hunter once."*

It stood seven to eleven metres from your bed after dark, pale and faintly lit, wearing the same
body as the lights. It greeted you before you reached it: *"You. Closer. I have waited long enough
to be quiet about it."*

> "I hunted this herd before you. The forest took me with the bow unstrung, and a hunter who never
> loosed is a poor thing to be for an age.
>
> Bring me what I lacked — ten flint and five scraps of leather, the quiver I never filled — and I
> will show you how the bow is strung. Come after dark. I walk in nothing else."

You brought ten flint and five leather scraps. It taught you the shape of the thing, and gave you
the one light it had kept.

> "Good. Now mark it: wood for the stave, resin to seal it, the herd's own hide for the wrap, and
> this flint of yours for what it looses.
>
> But no bench will strike it. It wants the sky, and there is one who waits on the shore when it
> rains who tends the only thing that can give it one. Take him a light — he will want one.
>
> Men will call it Thor's. Let them — the storm in it is the herd's, and Eikthyr's own, turned.
> What you loose from it, you owe the herd a clean shot."

### The race

Eikthyr would not answer a living stranger for free. His trophies were the price of an audience,
and trophies meant hunting his herd. So you did what the forest did, with one difference: what you
did with their lights was between you and the forest.

You hunted after dark. A deer fell, and its light rose where it fell and burned for half a minute.
The forest sent its children for it: a pack at the carcass at once, and more walking in out of the
dark towards the light itself. They were not vicious. They were starving. Every greydwarf is a
splinter of the Elder, and it goes dark unless it is fed.

*"Its light rises. Take it before they do — walk into it."* You ran into it at a sprint. The
scoreboard kept the truth in the open, you against the forest, and the boss drums played, because
this was the fight the act was about. Some nights a stray kindled that no deer had died for, one
the forest had not caught yet: *"Somewhere out in the dark, a stray light kindles."* Those came
quietly, with no pack.

You took back five. Had the forest taken eight, the hunt would have ended paying nothing: *"The
lights are gone, and the trophies with them. Hunt your own."* It did not come to that.

### The one who waits

*"When the rain comes there is someone standing at the water's edge. He has been there since before
the herd, he is not one of the forest's, and he has a name."*

He stood on the shore facing the water, and only while it rained. He was gold and larger than the
shade, and he spoke first: *"You walk like someone who has taken something. Come here."*

> "I broke a bone once. That is the whole of it — a goat's hind leg, snapped for the marrow, because
> I was hungry and it was lying there and I did not think anyone counted bones.
>
> He counted. He took me instead of the goat, and payment does not get to stop being payment just
> because the debt has gone old.
>
> So I know exactly what you are. You have been taking light that something else was counting. The
> difference between us is not the taking. It is that you still have somewhere to put it."

Thjalfi wanted twenty stone and one of the lights, carried in your hands. A light in a chest at home
was not in your hands. *"He wants paying, and what he does with it is the only reason he is still
here."* You paid.

> "Stone for the body. A light for the eye.
>
> Stand back.
>
> There. It is not a forge — there is no fire in it, and nothing in this world can make one worth
> the name. It only breaks. But put in a shape it knows and what it breaks is the shape's own edges,
> and what stands up out of it is the thing you meant.
>
> Put in anything else and you get ash. I have been getting it wrong for a very long time."

The Storm-Anvil stood on the shore at his feet. In the rain you put in ten wood, ten resin, six
deer hide, ten flint and three rescued lights, all in the box together, and pulled the lever.
*"The herd paid for this in hide, and the sky will pay the rest. Owe them a clean shot."* **Thor's
bow** came out. Its arrows struck with lightning.

### The Breaker

> "Something came out of the forest, and it is not collecting."
>
> "That one was never fed. It stopped carrying light a long time ago, and started breaking it."

*"The forest sent nothing for this one. It comes for the light itself, and it does not collect."* It
was a troll, a splinter that never reached the Elder's table. It had fifteen minutes before it moved
on, and it would take your house down if you fought it near anything you had built. For one fight
the forest was on your side:

> "The forest turns on it. They are not helping you — there is just something here they hate more.
> Stay clear of both."

You put it down, and kept its hide and the trophies it had been carrying. The shade came back after
dark to say so: *"Stay a moment. I have been thinking about what you did."*

> "You put it down. Good.
>
> It was one of theirs once — a splinter nothing ever came to collect, so it stopped carrying light
> and started breaking it. That is what the forest does to the ones it forgets. Something smaller
> forgot me, and it took longer.
>
> Keep the hide. Whatever the sky did to that thing, it stopped caring a long age ago, and the hide
> is where it stopped caring. Bound over a meadow frame, with light under the boss, it will do the
> same for you — but no bench will bind it either. That wants the shore, and the rain, and three of
> your lights."

So, back on the shore in the rain: twenty wood, twenty resin, ten troll hide, ten deer hide and
three rescued lights, exact, because the anvil burns whatever it does not recognise. *"The storm is
his. Bind something that does not care."* The **Stormward** came out, the biggest object a player
can hold. You let it answer three times. You blocked twice inside five seconds, and it gave the storm
back in a five-metre ring. *"It keeps what it is hit with. Let something hit it twice, and stand
still."* Each storm wore it down, and you learned to keep a bench at home.

Then you went out under a thunderstorm with no roof over you and the shield on your arm, because
*"The sky does this on its own, and has since before you landed. Go and be under it holding what you
made."*

### The Herald and the Gatherer

The herd's guardian was a named, starred deer, found by real bearings. *"Eikthyr's Herald falls."*
Its fall was the loudest light the meadows had ever released. Eikthyr's own rally answered it at the
carcass: *"The meadows heard that."*

Something else heard it too. *"The Gatherer knows what you have taken."* The oldest splinter, fat
enough to have a name, had followed the hunt all act and let its kin do the carrying. It came at
night, and it came as the race had made it. Every light you had let the forest take was health it
walked in with:

> "The Gatherer comes lean and furious. You left it nothing."

You killed it, and every light it had held went free around the corpse, yours to gather at leisure.

### Eikthyr

His altar was two standing stones ringed with runes. You hung the trophies his own herd had paid for
and called him down. Whether he saw a defiler or the only one who had fought the real thieves, the
saga never said.

He went out. Something went into you as he fell:

> "The way of the Hunter deepens: Menagerie [0], Elemental Arrows [→]."

Hugin said it, and so did the centre of the screen, because the first time it was only a raven,
nobody noticed. Thor's bow could cycle lightning, fire and frost now, and the menagerie answered
Keypad0. The thane, when you passed his graves again, had nothing left to hand over:

> "Nothing more yet. What they knew came to them slowly, and it comes to you the same way. When a god
> falls, the rest will find you where you stand."

The shade came back one last time.

> "You called him down, and he came, and he is gone.
>
> I hunted his herd a whole season and never once saw him. That is the difference between us and it
> is not skill — I never had a reason worth his coming. Keep the bow. The storm in it was his. Now it
> is only yours."

> *Eikthyr came down onto stones his own herd had paid for, and went out. You walked away carrying
> light this world had already lost once - the first of it ever to come back. The forest went on
> being hungry. Nobody had yet asked where it had been carrying everything it took.*

## Act II — Where the Light Goes

*"The forest has been fed for years. Meet what did the feeding."*

> "The little ones carry it somewhere. Nobody has ever followed them home. Odin would like that
> corrected." — Hugin

Twelve seconds after the card faded, the act said its premise once:

> "Nothing here makes its own light. Remember that."

You hung Eikthyr's trophy on the sacrificial stones and claimed his power. A stag's stride came when
you pressed the key and ran. Then you headed away from the meadows, into dark pines and rock. *"The
Gatherer was only a hand. Everything it collected went this way, for years."*

You took the antler pickaxe to the mottled outcrops, forty strokes into the Black Forest, and copper,
tin and coal came back. You hung a trophy on a wall and raised a raft, because the Elder does not
always wait on your shore. Then you raised a cart. *"Goods have to move. The couriers know it, and so
does the trader."*

### The burial chambers

*"The forest did not take every light. Some were carried down and buried, and the dead have been
keeping them lit ever since."*

You found stone doors set into the forest hills, ringed by standing stones, and went down. Green
fires burned in the walls. The cores were what burned in them, and you needed ten for a smelter. You
took them.

When you came out, someone was waiting at the door. He was ash-grey, lit from inside with the same
green as the walls. *"You took from the walls. Come here."*

> "You have been inside. I know, because the walls are dimmer.
>
> Those were ours. Every green fire down there is a light somebody buried rather than let the little
> ones carry it off. We could not keep ourselves, so we kept those, and we have kept them lit for
> longer than this forest has had a name.
>
> Burn them, if you must. The living need fire; I remember that much. But bring one back — one of
> the lights the little ones are carrying. They run at night. Cut one loose, carry it down to me, and
> I will give you what the storm left buried here."

You built the smelter anyway. *"The cores burn as well in a smelter as in a wall. Know what you are
burning."* Copper and tin went in, and bronze came out, the first thing in this forest anyone had
made instead of taken. You forged three things in it.

### The couriers

> "The forest moves its harvest at night."
>
> "Watch for the couriers — one of them will be carrying it, and the light warms whatever holds it.
> Cut that one down and its cargo rises."

Before you could rob anyone you had to clear the way. The shamans kept to the nests and healed what
you wounded, and the nests died without them. Then came the Brutes. *"The big ones are not stronger.
They are FED. The oldest trees feed first, and so do the oldest splinters."* You felled three, slow
and unflinching, where there was room to give ground.

Then you went out at night. They were starred and named, Couriers of the Elder, hurrying through the
dark, and the brand was the cargo. *"The courier falls, and its cargo rises. Walk into it."* It was
Act I's race turned around: the forest's kin came to carry the light onward, and you took it first.
Four lights, robbed off the supply line.

You carried one of them down to the barrow-keeper. *"One light. Carried down, not dug up."*

> "There. It will burn longer down here than it would have burned in him.
>
> The storm left a piece of itself in this forest, long ago, and we buried it with the rest. Bronze,
> troll hide and coal, at a forge. Strike it and it is yours."

*"The storm gave you a shield. That was the first piece. There is one waiting in every land left."*
At the forge, eight bronze, six troll hide and ten coal made the **Stormsworn helm**, the storm's
piece the forest had buried. It answers everything there that swings.

You built a portal, with fine wood, greydwarf eyes and surtling cores, and then its twin. *"The
couriers walk every mile of their road. You need not."* You broke ground with a cultivator and sowed
ten seeds.

### Haldor

*"Somebody in this forest has watched the couriers longer than you have. He sells things, at the
forest's edge."*

Haldor kept a camp in the Black Forest, and he did not move. Near him his chatter was not the usual
patter:

> "They come past every night. Little ones, glowing like lanterns. Never once stop to trade."
>
> "Funny thing. They carry more than they could ever eat, and they never look at my wares."
>
> "I keep my lamps low after dark. Not for the little ones. For the big ones."
>
> "Want to know where the light goes? I've watched them for years. It'll cost you a troll's head."

Splinters carry light and do not take goods, which is why a trader in a starving forest is never
robbed. The only thing he feared was a troll, the one thing that breaks light instead of carrying
it. You killed one in the forest, put its head on your hotbar, and pressed the key while looking at
him.

> "Ha! That one won't be knocking my camp over. Fair's fair, then. Every night they go the same way —
> deeper, to where the trees are oldest. There's a ring of stone in there, and something under it
> that's never once come up to collect. I've marked it on your map."

The pin was there.

### The Elder

*"Follow where the stolen light was carried — the oldest trees feed first. A ring of stone,
guarded."* You found it and called down the thing the couriers had been feeding: a hunger wearing a
tree. It burned.

> "The way of the Hunter deepens: Unseen [Ins]."

That was the third rung, and the last of Eydís.

At the burial chamber, the keeper had one more thing to say:

> "Whatever he was fed went out with him. The forest is darker for it.
>
> Not down here. Ours are still burning."

> *The Elder burned, and everything it had been fed went out with it. So that was where the light
> had gone: nowhere. Spent on keeping deadwood standing. The world was darker than the day you
> landed, and you had helped.*

## Act III — Nothing Stays Buried *(as designed — not yet built)*

*"What the marsh takes, it keeps."*

> "Men dug here before you, and iron is all they left. Take the iron. Leave the rest where it lies."
> — Hugin

You hung the Elder's trophy where you had hung Eikthyr's and claimed his power, faster felling for
the iron ahead. You built a fermenter with honey and herbs that came for it. Then you went out onto
flat, flooded, grey ground with poison mead in your pack. You cleared the mire of leeches in the
water, blobs on the paths, and draugr wherever the dead had been left. *"The swamp does not let go
of its dead"*: some of what you put down got up again as bone, once, right where it fell.

### The Bog Witch

*(The lines in this section are the design's meaning; her words are not written yet.)*

There was one living thing in the marsh, and she kept a camp there. Her talk was about the place:
nothing here rots because nothing here lets go, and she lives here because nothing keeps hold of
her. Bonemass, she said, is decided before the fight, by poison and the mead against it. So you
stirred a poison mead base at the cauldron and set it to ferment. Her shop stayed her own.

### The drowned one

*"Men dug here before you. The iron they left is the only thing down there still worth carrying."*
You hauled twenty scrap iron out of the muddy piles in the sunken crypts, opened with the Elder's
key.

At the nearest crypt door, standing in the shallows, there was a draugr who had kept his wits. He
was pale and waterlogged and lit a cold green-grey, and he did not attack. He had held that door
since before the iron rusted, because holding is all the marsh lets anyone do. What he wore was no
use to him now. When he had said so, the forge would make it: the **Stormsworn cuirass**, twelve
iron, six guck and ten leather scraps at an improved forge. *"Third piece. The men who left this
iron in the water did not have one."* The fen stopped getting into you.

Then he asked the one thing the marsh never does. Put me down.

He was not tame any more. He raised his weapon once, out of habit, at a fraction of his strength.
When he fell, the swamp did not take him back, and a light rose where he lay: the one he had never
let go of.

### The Stormward, reforged

You sailed the fens, six hundred metres of water inland, where most crypts sit on a shore. Then you
went back to the Bog Witch with the Stormward, ten iron and ten ancient bark, and asked her for the
one thing her shop does not sell. She took all three and handed back the **Ironbound Stormward**,
heavier and harder, still answering being hit with the storm. *Act I's capstone, re-made in the
place that keeps everything.*

### Bonemass

You felled an Abomination. *"Roots and drowned men, standing up together. This is what holding on
looks like."* Then you found a skull on a mound, deep in the mire, where the water hides the path,
and called down everything that had never let go. It came apart in water it had stood in since
before the herd.

> *Bonemass came apart in water it had stood in since before the herd. Nothing in the marsh had been
> carrying light anywhere. It had simply been kept - and that was the first hoard you had found that
> nobody was using.*

## Act IV — The White Silence

*"Above the treeline, even light freezes."*

> "Nothing up here is hungry. That is worse. Hunger at least wants something." — Hugin

You claimed Bonemass's power on the same stones, resistance to blunt, slash and pierce, because the
mountains would ask for it. You climbed above the snowline with frost mead and warm hide, since frost
kills without them.

You hunted the white silence. Wolves came, and fenrings at night, downhill. Wolf armour, frost arrows
and dragon eggs came of it. Boulders that were not boulders stood up beside you. *"They were set to
watch something. It has been gone a very long time. Nobody told them."* You killed two Stone Golems.

*"Nothing up here has moved in an age. Whatever the mountain was keeping, it is under your feet."*
Bonemass's wishbone rattled over silver buried in the rock, and you dug up fifteen ore. At a level
three forge, fourteen silver, six wolf pelts and four wolf fangs made the **Stormsworn greaves**.
*"Fourth piece, and the cold is the easiest thing it will ever have to keep out."*

On the highest bone-ringed peak you found Moder's altar and called her down.

> *Moder fell out of her own sky and the mountain gave up what it had been keeping. It was still warm.
> Whatever is up there is not taking light to use it, and that was worse than any answer you had had
> so far.*

## Act V — The Golden Ruin

*"They harvested a god's herd before you. See how it ended."*

> "Look at the stones before you fight anything. Someone tried exactly what you are trying." — Hugin

Moder's power gave you a following wind, whichever way you sailed. The plains were tall golden grass,
and the deathsquitos arrived before you heard them. Padded armour was handed to you for exactly that.
You broke the plains on deathsquitos and on lox that hit like a cart, and came away with black metal,
needle arrows, a blade and a lox cape.

*"They are still keeping a quota. Nothing has been harvested here in an age, and nobody has told them
that either."* You killed two Fuling Berserkers, two-handed and unstoppable, and took Yagluth's
totems from what was left.

*"The last piece. Wear all of it into what is left — that is what it was for."* At a level three
workbench, four lox pelts, ten needles and six silver made the **Stormsworn mantle**. Helm, cuirass,
greaves, mantle and the storm's own shield: the set was a record of what the run had survived. You
built a windmill on flat open ground to grind barley for the last feast.

Yagluth's altar was a ruin of stone hands in the tall grass, with fulings camped near it.

> *Yagluth's hand closed on nothing and stayed shut. The first harvesters were dust in the fields
> they had cleared, and you were standing in their answer. It had not worked for them either.*

That is where a saga ends unless the final god is moved further on. Its last reward reads, simply,
**"The saga is complete."** Every loan was repaid, and the score was how brightly you burned.

## The ending, as planned *(not yet built)*

Each act was a failed answer to the same shortage. The forest stole light, and spent it on
nothing. The marsh kept it, and never used it. The mountain froze around it, still warm. The plains
harvested it at scale, and the harvesters were dust. Only the dvergr carry light honestly, borrowed
in lanterns and handed back.

The wisp you pocketed from every race is the same kind of thing the dvergr carry. By the Mistlands
you would find out you had been doing it all along, since the first night in the meadows: taking
back what the forest was carrying, and keeping it lit. The dvergr's answer, practised before it was
taught. The last act spends it, where light goes to end.

The shortage is the saga, and only the end answers it. The ravens watched all of it, and never once
helped.
